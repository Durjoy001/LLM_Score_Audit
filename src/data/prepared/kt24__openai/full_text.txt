What we’re trying to solve:
Sep 13 2025 - Oct 13 2025 (30
For many Muslims in the West, faith is a central part of life. A Pew days)
Research Center study (2023–2024) found that 60% of U.S. Muslim adults
consider religion "very important." Yet the realities of balancing work,
family, and personal responsibilities often make acts of worship

challenging to maintain, and striving for spiritual excellence even more so.
Limited time, scarce resources, and emotional or physical exhaustion are
all variables that impact consistent engagement in faith-based practices,
which are essential for deepening spiritual connection, building resilience,
and fostering a sense of purpose in daily life.
Research shows that for young Muslims, these challenges can feel even
heavier. Many struggle with feelings of inadequacy or shame over their
religious shortcomings, especially in secular environments where Islam is
less visible. Without consistent ways to engage in acts of worship, it is
easy to feel spiritually disconnected, discouraged, and emotionally burnt
out. Evidently, this points to a clear need for practical tools and structured
support that help Muslims integrate faith into their daily lives with greater
ease.
The question then becomes: how can we make Allahʼs presence tangible
in daily life and support consistent spiritual development?
How we’re trying to solve it:
The Prophet ﷺ taught us that after the obligatory acts, nothing brings a
servant closer to Allah than voluntary deeds (Hadith Qudsi, Bukhari).
These nawāfil practices not only compensate for our shortcomings in
obligatory worship but also strengthen our connection to Allah, drawing
His love, mercy, and protection. Yet many Muslims struggle to sustain them
in the midst of busy, modern lives.
The Ihsan App was built to address this need. It is not just another Islamic
knowledge app, it is a practical tool designed to transform knowledge into
action and bring faith alive in daily routines.
Our team has carefully identified the wide range of voluntary acts
recommended in Islam and woven them into a unique digital experience

Where we are now:

Risks and challenges
1. Financial Sustainability
As a nonprofit organization, Ihsan has no paid features or monetization
strategy by design, in order to maintain accessibility for all users.
This creates a dependency on external funding (donations, grants,
sponsorships) to cover ongoing development, hosting, and maintenance
costs.
Without securing sufficient funding, there is a risk that two years of
development efforts could be lost, and the app may not be able to continue
operating post-launch.
2. Reliance on Sustainers
The long-term success of Ihsan depends on establishing a consistent base
of sustainers—individuals and organizations who are willing to contribute
regularly.
Failure to cultivate this supporter base could result in financial instability
and inability to scale or improve the platform.
3. Opportunity Cost of Non-Monetization
By foregoing traditional revenue models such as subscription fees or in-
app purchases, Ihsan is forgoing potential self-sustaining revenue streams.
While this aligns with the mission of maximizing accessibility, it heightens
reliance on external donors to keep the app operational.

The app is built with scalability and durability in mind. Rather than being a
short-term campaign tool, Ihsan is designed to remain useful over the long
run by continuously providing Muslims with daily reminders, contextual
duas, and acts of worship.
The content and functionality are evergreen—meaning they wonʼt expire or
become irrelevant quickly—ensuring that development resources invested
today continue to have an impact for years.
Reusability and recyclability
Core features and content modules (e.g. reminders, duas, acts of worship)
can be adapted and repurposed across multiple contexts and campaigns,
reducing the need for redundant redevelopment.
Educational and spiritual resources shared on the platform can be “reused”
by users themselves—passed on, taught to others, or applied in different
life situations—multiplying impact without additional material costs.
Sustainable materials
As a digital product, Ihsan eliminates the need for physical materials (e.g.
printed books, pamphlets, or reminder cards), thereby reducing paper
consumption, ink usage, and related waste.
The choice to deliver content digitally is itself a sustainable material
decision—prioritizing data and cloud infrastructure over consumable,
physical resources
Sustainable Distribution
The app can be distributed globally through app stores with no incremental
environmental cost per user. Unlike shipping physical goods, distribution
has near-zero marginal environmental impact.
Updates and improvements are delivered electronically, avoiding
packaging, shipping, and manufacturing emissions.
By centralizing spiritual reminders into one app, Ihsan reduces the need for
fragmented physical alternatives, supporting a lighter environmental
footprint.
