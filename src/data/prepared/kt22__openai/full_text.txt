The real reason I'm building this
═══════════════════════════════════════
When the AI engines first came out, I knew immediately that something
had
shifted. Not because the model was good. It wasn't, not really,
not yet but because for the first time I could talk to a machine
that seemed to understand what I was saying.
So I started building. I used every system I could get my hands on —
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok — pushing each of them as far as
they'd go. And every single one had the same wall: the moment I
closed the tab, the system forgot I existed. Whatever we'd built
together, whatever insight we'd reached, whatever it had learned
about how I think, gone.
Two years in, I stopped thinking of memory as a feature.
I started thinking of it as a precondition.
You cannot build a mind that resets every Tuesday. You cannot
evolve something that begins again at zero with every conversation.
Whatever artificial general intelligence is going to be, when it
arrives, it will have woken up inside a system that remembered
yesterday.
InsightAI started as that bet. It's still that bet. It's a
sovereign, self-hosted cognitive substrate built around the premise
that memory and continuity aren't optional they're the floor.
The minimum. The thing without which nothing else matters.
Will InsightAI become AGI? I don't know. I'm not promising you
that. What I am promising is that it has the architectural shape
that cloud AI doesn't and that the people who back this campaign
will own their copy of it before that question gets answered.
I'd rather be wrong trying than right by accident.

I'll be honest about what could go wrong, because the worst thing
a Kickstarter founder can do is pretend the path is clear.
────────────────────────────────────────
Timeline risk
────────────────────────────────────────
I'm a solo technical founder. The platform is at roughly 90%
production readiness today, but the final 10% — installer
polish across three operating systems, onboarding, and
production hardening — historically takes longer than
founders predict. My realistic timeline is 4–6 months
post-funding to ship. If I hit a wall I can't see from here,
I will tell backers directly through campaign updates rather
than quietly slip the date.
────────────────────────────────────────
Technical risk
────────────────────────────────────────
InsightAI runs locally, which means it depends on each
backer's hardware. The platform is designed for modern
machines (Apple Silicon, recent Nvidia/AMD GPUs, or
sufficient CPU + RAM for CPU-only mode), but performance
will vary. I will publish minimum and recommended hardware
specs before fulfillment so no backer is surprised. Backers
whose hardware doesn't meet specs will be offered a refund
or a transferable license.
────────────────────────────────────────
Model dependency risk
────────────────────────────────────────
InsightAI runs on open-weight models — currently Llama 3.3
70B with a Llama 3.1 8B fallback. If Meta or another
provider materially changes their license terms in a way
that affects redistribution, I will migrate to whichever
open-weight model best preserves the architecture.
Open-weight alternatives are growing, not shrinking, and
the platform is model-agnostic by design.

Solo founder risk
────────────────────────────────────────
I am one person. If I'm hit by a bus, the project is at

architecture is mapped, and post-funding I will deposit a

The bigger question
────────────────────────────────────────
Some people will read the project page and worry that the
AGI ambition means I'm overpromising what InsightAI will
become. I want to address this directly: I am not promising
that InsightAI will become AGI. I am promising that it has
the architectural shape — persistent memory, expert
specialization, behavioral learning, sovereign deployment —
that I believe is required for AGI to emerge. Whether that
emergence happens, and on what timeline, is genuinely
unknown. What backers receive on day one is a working
sovereign cognitive AI. Anything beyond that is a bet we're
making together.

Long-lasting design
InsightAI is built for permanence, not obsolescence.
Three deliberate architectural choices extend the life of
what backers receive far beyond a typical software product:
1. Self-hosted, no server dependency. The platform runs
entirely on the user's own hardware. There is no central
server that can be shut down, no cloud service that can
be sunset, and no subscription that can lapse. The
software a backer receives on day one will continue to
function indefinitely, regardless of the company's
future.
2. Open-weight model foundation. InsightAI is built on
open-weight large language models (Llama 3.3 70B / 3.1
8B), not closed APIs. This means backers are never at
the mercy of a vendor deprecating an API or revoking
access. The models live on disk, locally, and run
without external dependency.
3. Documented, transferable architecture. The codebase is
thoroughly documented and the architecture is mapped in
public diagrams. As part of my commitment to backers,
I will deposit a release-of-source clause with a third
party so that, in the unlikely event the project cannot
continue, lifetime-license holders retain perpetual
access to the source code and the right to maintain
their own copies.
Compared to subscription cloud AI — which by design has a
finite life tied to vendor business decisions — InsightAI

Sustainable Distribution
InsightAI is delivered as a 100% digital product. There is
nothing to ship, nothing to package, and nothing to fly
across an ocean.
Specifically:
- Zero physical packaging. No boxes, no plastic, no
printed manuals, no shipping inserts.
- Zero transportation footprint. Backers download the
software directly from a CDN; there is no warehouse,
no fulfillment center, no last-mile delivery truck.
- Zero returns logistics. Software does not need to be
returned, repaired, or replaced through reverse-logistics
channels.
- Local-first inference. Once installed, InsightAI runs
on hardware the user already owns. Unlike cloud AI —
which routes every query through hyperscale data
centers consuming significant power and water — local
inference adds no incremental data-center load per use.
The most sustainable shipping route is the one that doesn't
exist. By designing InsightAI as a sovereign, locally-run,
digitally-delivered product, the campaign's environmental
footprint per backer is bounded almost entirely by the
energy used to download a single installer file.
For digital infrastructure I do control (the download CDN,
the project website, supporting services), I prioritize
providers with verifiable renewable-energy commitments.
