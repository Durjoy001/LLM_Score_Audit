How Travel Planner V3 Started
I have always tracked and shared my travel configurations with the other
travelers, but things get messy and complicated with no standards. There
was simply no acceptable tool for our needs, even including some
popular trip/itinerary planners such as TripIt.
Built in Microsoft Excel (note: required to use Travel Planner V3) as a
macro-enabled workbook, I am in the process of excitingly creating this to
share with all travelers.

The premise is simple -- you go somewhere, whether via flight or
otherwise, and return. But it gets confusing on where to go, how to plan,
and especially how to budget. Travel Planner V3 is a budget-oriented
tool that reverses the common method of inputting all of your
supporting information into websites, where it's often lost later, then
sloppily transferring to your notes, copying all your email reservations, or
simply no saving of information at all. With inputting your supporting travel
configurations into a single, unified tool, everything is stored! Travel
Planner V3 focuses on easily planning, budgeting, saving, and sharing
your travel configurations with others.
The tool helps from the initial planning stages of a trip all the way until after
your trip. You can check out more of it's features further below.
---------------------------------
Where the Funding is Going
The mock-ups and some basic functions are complete but there still
needs plenty of refinement, VBA coding, and testing. Moreover, this
application is currently being developed for Canadians (CAD pricing), but if
funding is complete, I will also make a USA version (USD pricing). Global
users can still use the application, but will have to convert (or navigate the
website) to local currency -- otherwise all functions remain the same!

Risks and challenges
[RISK = LOW] Testing often finds errors, and some more challenging than
others. If an application-breaking bug is found and cannot be resolved
without sacrificing features, the scope of the application may be changed
slightly.
[RISK = LOW] Travel Planner V3 is being designed for newer laptop and
widescreen displays. Because it uses a concept of "frozen panes" for the
navigation sidebar, if your screen resolution is lower than 1280x800
(optimally 1600x900) then you may experience cells that cannot be viewed
as intended. This is generally not an issue for laptops built after 2008.
