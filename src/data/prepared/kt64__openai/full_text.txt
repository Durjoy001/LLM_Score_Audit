As an aspiring entrepreneur with no coding skills, my journey toward
creating a QR code-based mobile payment application was nothing short Jan 2 2025 - Mar 3 2025 (60
of a rollercoaster. It all began during a trip to a local café, where I days)
witnessed a long line of frustrated customers waiting to pay with cash or
cards. There had to be a better way to make payments faster, simpler, and
more convenient for everyone. It was at that moment that the idea struck

me: a mobile payment app using QR codes to streamline the process,
allowing people to make payments with just a scan.
Despite the excitement of the idea, I was faced with a massive obstacle: I
didnʼt know a thing about coding. I had no technical background and had
never written a line of code in my life. But the more I thought about it, the
more I realized that technology was advancing rapidly, and if I didnʼt jump
in, someone else would. My first step was to educate myself about the
problem I was trying to solve and how QR codes were being used in the
payment industry.
I dove into the world of digital payments, learning about how QR codes
work, the benefits of contactless transactions, and the security concerns
associated with them. I wasnʼt aiming to become a coder, but I wanted to
understand the process well enough to communicate my vision to others.
With this knowledge in hand, I started sketching my idea. I mapped out
how the app would work: users could simply scan a QR code to make a
payment, and the money would transfer securely to the merchantʼs
account. I designed a simple user interface (UI) that would be intuitive,
easy to navigate, and secure—key features for building trust with users.
I wanted to create an app that would make payments as simple as
scanning a code on your phone. I worked closely so the appʼs design and
user experience matched my vision.
I actively participated in the development process by working on the user
journey and identifying potential pain points. I created user personas and
gathered feedback from friends and acquaintances who represented the
typical app users—people who were busy, tech-savvy but not necessarily
experts, and looking for a hassle-free way to pay.
