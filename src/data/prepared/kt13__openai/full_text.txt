Story

In 1988, Steve Dorner wrote an eMail client that soon grew to have an
army of very satisfied users. It was named "Eudora" in honour of
American authoress Eudora Welty, who wrote a short story called "Why
I Live at the P.O." Eudora quickly gained a legion of fans and was
bought out by Qualcomm in 1991. Fast forward to 2006 and, amidst
shifting corporate priorities, Qualcomm Eudora was killed off, to be
replaced by a pale imitation called Eudora OSE. This was not well
received and soon died as well.
Eudora still has its fans, though, which is why we're bringing it back!
This year, its code was open-sourced and given to the Computer
History Museum. Seeing that nobody has taken on the monumental
task of reviving this excellent piece of software and updating it to
comply with today's standards, I picked up development myself, along
with a talented team of C++ programmers.
Legal encumbrances prevented us from using the Eudora name, so the
program has been re-christened Hermes, after the Greek
personification of business and patron deity of messengers. Make no
mistake, though, it's still the same Eudora you know and love. It's the
"real McCoy"---no imitation and certainly no OSE.

The main challenge with complling and delivering Hermes is that, as
written by Qualcomm, it depends on a customised user interface toolkit
known as RogueWave Stingray and will not compile without it. This is
commercial software and could not be distributed with the source code;
that said, native UI toolkits are now much more powerful than they were
in 2005, and RogueWave can therefore be replaced with WxWidgets.
There *will* be a version for Mac and another version for Linux. We plan
to support all major computing platforms.
As originally written, Hermes (then known as Eudora) used Microsoft
Trident for its HTML eMail viewer/editor. This is the same engine as
displays Web pages in Internet Explorer, a software package that is now
deprecated. It will have to be replaced, most probably with Gecko (its
Firefox equivalent). Spell check will also have to be replaced, as will the
unsolicited mail ("junk") filter (preferably with a Bayesian alternative,
e.g. SpamAssassin or Bogofilter).
That said, we have a talented team of developers that should be able to
get this project off the ground and delivered Real Soon Now! Look out
for the QCSSL dll required for Eudora to communicate with modern SSL
servers!
