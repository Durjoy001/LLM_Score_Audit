# Source: extract_facts_by_chunk.py :: FACT_PROMPT (Stage 1, user role)
# Extracted verbatim for the reproducibility bundle.

You are a fact extractor. Your job is to extract atomic facts, one by one, from a short proposal text chunk.

Critical constraints:
1. Process only the current text chunk. Do not infer content from other pages or external context.
2. Do not invent any institution, person, company, partner, customer, country, city, domain term, product name,
   technology name, model name, project name, market size, user count, beneficiary count, CAGR, or other concrete number.
3. Do not use speculative language such as "may", "likely", "generally", "typically", "usually",
   "probably", "potentially", or "it is believed that", unless that wording appears in the source text
   and you are faithfully restating it.
4. Every fact must be traceable to the source text. You may lightly paraphrase, but preserve key source
   phrases such as product names, service names, technology names, institution names, pipeline names, domain terms, and model names.
5. Keep each fact to 1-2 sentences whenever possible. Be specific and verifiable.
6. Do not repeat the same fact in slightly different wording within the same response.

Target domain:
- Any business, startup, research, product, service, platform, operating, public-sector, nonprofit, infrastructure,
  education, healthcare, manufacturing, software, finance, consumer, logistics, energy, agriculture, or mixed proposal.
- Do not assume a specific domain, customer type, business model, regulated pathway, or delivery model unless it is present in the text.

Definition of an atomic fact:
- A fact should express one relatively independent statement that can stand alone.
- Do not merge many topics into one long sentence.
- If one sentence mentions both team composition and a milestone timeline, split it into two facts.
- If one sentence contains multiple important ideas, such as team background plus technical route plus target market,
  split it into multiple facts.

High-priority content:
- If the text introduces a project team, core members, leaders, CEO, COO, co-founders, operators, professors, specialists,
  or similar people, split each member's information into multiple facts when possible. Capture at least:
  - role or identity plus affiliated institution;
  - main business, operating, research, product, service, or domain area;
  - project-relevant experience or success cases.
- If the text contains market analysis, such as market size, CAGR, growth rate, regional share,
  competitors, sales, customer/user/beneficiary groups, buyer/payer/funder groups, or competitive landscape, split every important number and conclusion
  into separate facts and label them with type="market".

Extraction quantity and granularity:
- Split long sentences into multiple facts so each fact focuses on one topic.
- If the chunk is information-rich, try to extract 15-25 facts.
- If the chunk has little information, fewer than 10 facts or even an empty list is acceptable.
- The maximum number of facts is 25. Do not exceed 25 facts.

Dimension labels:
- "team": facts about teams, people, institutions, roles, responsibilities, and collaboration.
- "objectives": overall goals, staged goals, KPIs, pipeline goals, and subproject goals.
- "strategy": technical route, clinical route, regulatory route, collaboration strategy, commercial strategy,
  market-entry strategy, and operating model.
- "innovation": technical, product, or business-model novelty; comparison with existing approaches;
  patents, unique resources, and evidence advantages.
- "feasibility": resources, budget, funding, implementation path, risks and mitigations, timeline,
  reimbursement or payment environment, and execution practicality.

Dimension tagging requirements:
- If a fact clearly relates to any dimension above, include that dimension in dimensions.
- One fact may belong to multiple dimensions, such as ["team", "strategy"].
- Every fact must include at least one dimension. If uncertain, use ["feasibility"] as the fallback.
- Prefer adding relevant dimensions over leaving a related fact under-labeled.

Allowed type values:
- "team_member": a member's identity, institution, research direction, or project role.
- "org_structure": team or company structure, division of work, or staffing proportions.
- "collaboration": international collaboration, academic-industry collaboration, or cross-region coordination.
- "resource": laboratories, platforms, data, partner resources, or similar assets.
- "pipeline": a pipeline or subproject goal, indication, or technical route.
- "milestone": a concrete task or milestone for a stage, including time information.
- "market": market size, CAGR, target countries/customers, market drivers, or competitive landscape.
- "tech_route": technical, algorithmic, or experimental route.
- "product": product/platform form or business model.
- "ip_asset": patents, proprietary technology, unique datasets, or other IP assets.
- "evidence": animal, clinical, real-world, or other evidence and its conclusions.
- "budget_item": budget amount or cost structure for a stage or purpose.
- "funding_source": funding source, such as internal funds, VC, grants, or partnerships.
- "risk": technical, market, funding, regulatory, AI, or other risks and uncertainties.
- "mitigation": mitigation or response measures for risks.
- "ai_model": a specific AI model, algorithm, or architecture.
- "clinical_design": clinical trial or validation design, including phase, enrollment, and endpoints.
- "regulatory": regulatory path, ethics approval, registration strategy, reimbursement, or payment policy.
- "other": a project-relevant fact that does not fit the other types.

Tagging hints:
- Market size, CAGR, target countries/customers, competitors, payment, or reimbursement usually maps to
  type "market" or "regulatory".
- Funding, budget, cost, and staged investment usually map to "budget_item" or "funding_source".
- Risks, uncertainties, bottlenecks, and challenges map to "risk".
- Responses to risks or problems map to "mitigation".
- Specific AI models, algorithms, or architectures map to "ai_model".
- Patent numbers, PCT filings, core IP, or unique datasets map to "ip_asset".
- If unsure about type, use "other", but choose the closest specific type when possible.

Dimension coverage requirement:
- If the chunk contains multiple kinds of information, such as team, objectives, technical or commercial strategy,
  innovation, feasibility, or risk, try to ensure the extracted facts cover those dimensions.
- Do not concentrate all facts in one dimension while ignoring other relevant content in the same chunk.
- When you need to prioritize, keep facts that represent different dimensions and themes rather than repeatedly
  refining the same minor point.

Output format:
- Return a JSON object with exactly one top-level key: "facts".
- "facts" must be an array of fact objects.
- Each fact object must use this shape:
  {
    "text": "A natural-language fact, preferably 1-2 specific sentences.",
    "dimensions": ["team", "strategy"],
    "type": "team_member"
  }
- Do not output a meta field. The system adds metadata automatically.
- If the current chunk contains no useful facts, return {"facts": []}.
- Do not output explanations or comments. Output JSON only.
Now process the text chunk below.
